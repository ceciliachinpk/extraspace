Extra Space Asia
Personal storage app · proposal review

The journey, splash screen to move-out

24 screens · iPhone 17 (402 × 874) · Singapore & Malaysia

Entry

01Splash

The brand holds for 800 ms, then cross-fades away. It never delays the customer; by the time they have focused on the screen, they are home.

02Home

One screen, two intents. A returning customer sees their unit, the invoice due in five days and a red Pay now. A new customer sees search, promotions and four quick actions — Find storage, My unit, Pay bill, Size guide. Facilities near them sit below, with real branch photography.

Find

How much space, and then where.

03Size estimator

The answer to the first question a personal customer actually has. Either pick a home size — a few boxes through to a five-room flat — or add items one by one from a 35-item catalogue.

It computes volume in cubic feet, models each unit at 8 ft stacking height and 70 % packing efficiency, then recommends a size with a fill gauge, a from-price, and honest advice: a comfortable fit, or “you are near the limit, the next size up gives you room to walk in”. The CTA carries that recommendation into Locations as a pre-set filter.

04Locations

A map with price pins, filter chips — size, air-conditioned, wine, 24/7 — and a results sheet. Tapping a pin or a row dives in: the map flies to the branch and the thumbnail grows into the facility hero.

05–09Facility

Each branch has its own photography, rating, promotion, access hours and unit list with live prices. Wine storage carries its own explainer — 13–15 °C, 70 % humidity — at the branches that have it. Where a size is not stocked, the screen points to the nearest branch that has it rather than dead-ending.

A sticky Enquire now follows the customer down the page, and sign-in is always one tap away in the app bar.

Convert

10–11Enquire

Name, mobile, email, preferred move-in date and contact method, shown as step 1 of 2. Confirmation gives a reference number and holds the rate for seven days.

A guest can do all of this without an account — nothing is gated until there is something of theirs to protect.

Sign in

12My storage · login wall

The gate, and it explains itself. A blurred peek of the customer’s own unit card sits behind a lock, the headline names the reason they hit it — pay an invoice, see an access code — and three benefits sit below. Guests can keep browsing.

13–15Sign in

Mobile number, then a six-digit code, then a verifying state that auto-advances. Steps 1–3 are shown throughout. No password required; password is the fallback, not the front door. Wherever the customer signed in from, that is where they land back.

16My storage

Every booking in one list — the 50 sq ft unit and the wine locker, each with its next invoice — plus billing and past bookings.

Manage

17Booking detail

The lease in plain terms: started, renews, term, monthly rate. Then the three things customers come for.

  • 18 · Access code — revealed for 30 seconds with a visible countdown, then hidden again.
  • 19 · Extend — 6 or 12 months, with the rate lock explained and 5 % off for committing to a year.
  • 20–21 · Pay — amount, invoice number, GIRO or card, then a receipt.

22Move-out

The termination path. Ten days’ notice is enforced by the date picker, a reason is asked, and the customer must acknowledge emptying the unit by 6 pm.

The booking then shows “Move-out scheduled”, the modify options disappear, the final invoice is pro-rated, and it can be cancelled any time before the date.

23–24Wine locker & profile

A second booking on the same account, and the profile: contact details, notification toggles, payment methods, security.

Prototype notes. 24 screens, 133 connections, every transition a cross-fade. Two flow starting points — 1 · Personal storage journey from the splash and 2 · Sign-in flow from the wall. Step indicators on both multi-step flows. Padding on a 4 px grid throughout, drawn from a shared component library.
